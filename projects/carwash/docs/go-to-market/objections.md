# Objection handling

Honest answers, each limited to what the product does today.

**"I trust my staff."**
Then the numbers will agree and you will have a record that says so, which also protects your good
staff when a customer disputes a charge. It is not an accusation; flags are leads to check, not proof.

**"It's too expensive."**
KES 3,500 a month for one site (KES 3,000 each for two to five) is the whole price: no setup fee and no
share of your takings. The honest comparison is the size of the gap Forecourt finds at your wash. Run the
14-day trial and look at your own number; if it finds nothing, cancel. (Prices are provisional.)

**"My attendants can't use a phone app."**
There is one screen: the plate, tick the services, press Record job, then three buttons as the car moves.
Show them. If they cannot do it in a day, that is worth knowing too.

**"I don't have cameras or water meters."**
You do not need them to start. Without devices it compares recorded jobs against M-Pesa and cash and
catches unpaid jobs, payments with no job, underquoting, an unusual cash share and abandoned-job
patterns. Meters and cameras add cars-without-a-job and water-outside-hours checks. We do not supply
hardware today.

**"What about cash? They can just not record it."**
Cash is the weakest witness, and the product says so: declared cash is compared with the work and, if
you have a meter, the water, rather than trusted. A wash with high cash and low recorded work stands
out. If nothing independent witnesses a car, the product cannot see it.

**"Do M-Pesa payments match automatically?"**
They do once your till's confirmation URL is registered with Safaricom to point at Forecourt. That
needs a short session together and has not yet been tested with a real payment, so I will show you it
working on yours before you pay anything.

**"What happens to my data if I stop paying?"**
Your account goes read-only after the grace period. Your records stay, and your tills and phones keep
recording. Paying switches everything back on at once. We do not delete your records.

**"Is my data safe? Who sees it?"**
Customers are separated from each other in the database itself. Attendants cannot read payments, flags
or water readings. Passwords are never stored (PINs are hashed). We have not had an independent security
review, and there is no backup restore drill yet: I will not claim more than that.

**"Do I have to tell my staff? Is it legal?"**
It holds personal data (names, phone numbers, number plates). Tell your staff what is recorded and why.
Whether you need to register with the Data Protection Commissioner, and what notice is required, has not
been checked here: ask a lawyer.

**"What if it's wrong?"**
Flags can be wrong. Each shows its evidence, a manager can resolve it with a note, and nothing is
deleted. Treat a flag as the place to look, not the verdict.
