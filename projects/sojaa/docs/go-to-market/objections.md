# Objections and honest answers

**"Firms make money by underpaying guards. Why would an owner buy software that proves what they pay?"** This is the strongest objection and it is partly right. A firm that wants to keep paying below the minimum will not buy this, and we are not selling to it. Sojaa is for the firm that already pays the minimum or wants to, and that faces (a) enforcement it has seen in the news, (b) clients who audit their security supplier, (c) guards' unions naming firms, and (d) its own uncertainty about which guard is paid what. To that firm the compliance report is protection: it shows what you pay, who is short and by how much, and lets you fix it before an inspector asks. The research found no Kenyan price for guard software and said plainly that the absence of competitors is weak evidence; it also said selling "evidence" to a buyer who profits from its absence is a hard sale. Expect to sell to the compliant and the worried, not to everyone. Do not oversell it as a way to avoid an inspection: it records what happened and cannot make a record true.

**"Can't a supervisor just tap check-in for a guard who isn't there?"** Yes. Sojaa makes it harder to do quietly and easier to see: the phone's position is compared with the site (a check-in far away is flagged), QR patrols need someone at each checkpoint, a missed shift shows on the board, every correction needs a reason and keeps the original, and a client can see the verification summary. It cannot stop a supervisor and a guard who agree to lie. We say so up front.

**"Is the minimum wage KES 30,000 and is it going to stay?"** The research found a High Court ruling upholding KES 30,000 (February 2025) and press reports of a presidential enforcement order (6 August 2026); it also found the court once suspended the regulation before ruling the other way, so enforcement could reverse. Sojaa ships KES 30,000 as a starting figure **you** confirm, never as fact. Change it in settings when the order changes.

**"Does it calculate NSSF, SHA, housing levy and PAYE correctly?"** It applies the tables you enter and confirm. It ships an illustrative set so a trial is not empty; that set is **unverified** and loads as unconfirmed, and nothing is deducted with it until a named person confirms it. Check every figure against the official schedule. Sojaa does not remit or file anything.

**"Does it report to PSRA?"** No. No PSRA reporting format was found and none is assumed. It keeps records you can show.

**"Does it check my guards' PSRA registration?"** No. It keeps what you type and flags an expiry date you typed that has passed. There is no public service it could call to verify.

**"Our guards have basic phones and no data."** Check-in needs a browser and a network today, from the supervisor's phone or the guard's. There is no offline mode and no USSD or SMS check-in yet. A supervisor can record a missed tap afterwards with a reason. If most of your sites have no signal, wait.

**"We already use Excel / a patrol clock / a foreign tool."** Fine for a list. A patrol clock proves a round; it does not roster, pay or invoice. The foreign tools in the research are priced in dollars for US firms. Sojaa is priced in shillings per guard and built around Kenyan payroll lines. It is also new and has no customers yet: we say so.

**"What if you disappear?"** Everything exports to CSV: guards, attendance, payroll, invoices with their evidence, incidents, debtors.
