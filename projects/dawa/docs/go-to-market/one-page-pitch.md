# Dawa: a pharmacy till that knows every batch

**For:** an owner-run pharmacy or a small chain in Kenya. **Price (provisional):** KES 4,500 a month for the first branch, KES 3,500 for each extra, after a 14-day free trial. No setup fee, no share of your sales.

## The problem it answers
- Stock expires on the shelf and you find out when it is too late to return it.
- Cash and stock go missing and nobody can say when or who.
- Prescription and controlled-drug records are in a book, or not kept.
- Reporting rules are changing and you want your records ready, not scrambled together.

## What you get
1. **Never sell expired stock.** Every delivery is entered by batch and expiry. The till sells the soonest-expiring batch first and refuses expired stock. An overview shows what is expired, what expires in 90 days (so you can return it), and what is running low.
2. **Scan the pack.** The 2D code gives batch, expiry and serial automatically. A pack scanned twice, or one you never received, is stopped at the till.
3. **Records you can show.** Prescription items need the patient and the prescriber. Controlled drugs need a second person to confirm with their own PIN, with a running balance. None of it can be edited afterwards. Download any of it as a spreadsheet.
4. **Cash that adds up.** M-Pesa payments match their sales (or type the code). At day end each cashier's cash is counted against what the system expects, and the difference is on record.
5. **Know what sells.** Margin by product, fast and slow movers, money tied up in stock that is not selling, expiry loss, what you owe suppliers.

## Straight about the limits
- It is **not connected** to the government's medicine track-and-trace platforms: no interface has been published to us. It records what such a report would contain and exports it.
- It needs the internet at the till. No receipt printing yet. No transfers between branches yet.
- You decide how each medicine is classified; Dawa does not.
