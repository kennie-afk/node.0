# Objection handling (honest answers)

**"I already have a POS."** Ask what it does about expiry and about serial numbers. Many cheap tills do neither. If theirs already tracks batch and expiry and keeps the records they need, they may not need Dawa; say so. Offer the sample branch rather than arguing.

**"It is too expensive."** KES 4,500 a month is about KES 150 a day. Compare it with what expired stock cost them last month: Dawa's Overview shows this in money once one delivery is entered. If it does not show a loss worth that, do not push.

**"The government platform is free."** Yes, and they must register on it themselves. Dawa does not replace it and is not connected to it (no interface has been published). Dawa is the till and the records behind it. If the regulator ships a free dispensing tool, part of the reason to buy disappears; that is a real risk and it is fair to say so.

**"Will it connect to the government system?"** Not today, honestly. No specification exists for us to build against. It records the batch, expiry and serial data a report would need, and exports it, so the history is there when a connection is possible. Do not promise a date.

**"My internet goes down."** The till needs the network; there is no offline mode yet. Do not hide this. Ask how often the connection drops during opening hours; if it is often, Dawa is not ready for that shop yet.

**"Is my data safe? It is patient information."** Be careful here: it is health information. Access is by individual PIN, records cannot be edited after the fact, every pharmacy is isolated from every other at the database level, and the isolation is tested. But there has been no independent security review, no backup and restore drill, and no data-protection assessment yet. Say that, and say you will tell them when it changes.

**"My staff will not learn it."** The cashier screen is search, scan, take payment. Start with the sample branch for an afternoon. If a cashier cannot manage it in a day, that is feedback we want.

**"My stock is on paper."** The first delivery is the work: enter batch numbers and expiries (or scan packs). Offer to do the first one together. Alerts become true from that moment, not before.

**"What if I stop paying?"** The account becomes read-only after a grace period; nothing is deleted, and paying reopens it immediately. They can export dispensing records as a spreadsheet at any time.

**"Can I try it first?"** Yes: fourteen days free, and a sample branch to practise on that is never billed.
